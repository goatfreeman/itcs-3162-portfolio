THE QUESTION

What I wanted to understand

How did internet access change in Hong Kong, and how do user counts compare with cellular and broadband subscriptions?

THE WORKFLOW

From raw data to a testable view

I filtered the global country-year dataset to Hong Kong, then followed internet-user counts and three access indicators over time with line charts.

  3. 03

    Separate charts make the change over time visible while preserving the distinction between internet users and service subscriptions.

WHAT THE RUN SHOWED

Findings, with caveats.

Observed finding

The series shows a strong rise in internet use from the mid-1990s onward. Cellular subscriptions grow on a different scale and eventually exceed 100 per 100 people, so they cannot be read as a count of unique internet users.

What to keep in mind

These are aggregated country-level indicators. A subscription is not proof that a unique person has internet access, and the dataset’s definitions should be checked before comparing metrics directly.

Next iteration: A useful next step would be to verify indicator definitions and add population-normalized measures before making direct cross-metric comparisons.
